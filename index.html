A Personalized Education

Competency-Based Learning

At Matchbook Learning, we provide a rigorous, personalized, standards-aligned education. Core subjects — English Language Arts, Mathematics, Science, and Social Studies — are taught using high-quality, research-based materials aligned to Indiana Academic Standards (Savvas MyView, enVision Math, and MyPerspectives).

Every student's education is built around them, not a fixed pace or one-size-fits-all path. Students advance by demonstrating mastery — teachers track where each student stands and adjust instruction, pace, and support so every child works at the right level of challenge.

Our five competencies name the skills and character we want every Matchbook student to carry beyond our walls. They shape the proficiency scale used for grading and are the focus of each student's monthly conference.

Academic Ownership

I take charge of my own learning. I set goals, track my progress, reflect on how I'm growing, ask for help, and do my best work.

Collaboration

I work well with others toward a shared goal, and I value everyone's ideas.

Communication

I share my ideas clearly, and I listen to others and build on what they say.

Honor

I take responsibility for what I do, follow through on what I say, and treat my school community with respect.

Resilience

I keep trying even when something is hard, and I use feedback to get better.

05Attendance & Chronic-Absence Supports

Be Here. Be On Time.

Regular attendance is essential to student success. Students are expected to be present and on time every day; Indiana law requires school attendance for all students.

Absences

Report all absences to the main office by 9:00 AM on the day of the absence. A written note from a parent/guardian or physician is required upon return for an excused absence. Excused absences include illness, medical/dental appointments, family emergencies, and religious observances. Unexcused absences may result in academic consequences and are reported to the state; 10+ unexcused absences may result in referral to the attendance intervention team.

Tardiness & Chronic Absence

Students arriving after 7:35 AM must sign in at the main office. Excessive tardiness is addressed through the MTSS process and parent conferences. A student is chronically absent when they miss 10% or more of school days — and we work actively with families to identify and remove barriers.

07Support · MTSS, DBRC/CICO, Re-Entry

The Right Support,
At The Right Time

Matchbook uses a Multi-Tiered System of Support (MTSS) to match every student's needs to high-quality instruction, intervention, or enrichment — and to respond quickly.

Tier 1

All students receive schoolwide expectations, classroom routines, reteaching, and early family communication. Family role: reinforce Safe, Respectful, Responsible at home.

Tier 2

Some students receive targeted supports such as CICO, DBRC, mentor check-ins, and weekly review. Family role: attend the meeting and support the student's goal.

Tier 3

A few students need individualized planning, intensive support, behavior plans, or re-entry support. Family role: co-create the plan and attend review meetings.

09Student Code of Conduct

Teach. Repair.
Return To Learning.

Matchbook Learning is a restorative school community. We teach expectations, recognize growth, repair harm, and help students return to learning whenever possible.

Restorative practice does not mean there are no consequences. It means consequences should help students understand impact, take responsibility, repair when possible, and learn the next right step.

This Is A Family-Facing Summary

Matchbook follows the current applicable Student Code of Conduct for student behavior, discipline, due process, family notification, suspension, expulsion, appeal rights, and student support. If this section conflicts with the official Student Code of Conduct, board policy, law, or required student-rights procedures, the official Code of Conduct, policy, and law control. Families are expected to review the Student Code of Conduct and this handbook section with their student each year.

How Matchbook Responds To Behavior

The levels below help families understand how Matchbook generally responds to behavior. The response level depends on the seriousness of the behavior, safety impact, developmental factors, student needs, prior support, and applicable policy requirements. Serious or unsafe behavior may begin at Level 3 or Level 4 even if there has not been a prior pattern.

CODE-01 · How Matchbook Responds To Behavior
LevelWhat it meansPossible Matchbook response
Level 1Classroom or school-based behavior that can usually be corrected with teacher support.Reteaching, redirection, private correction, restorative conversation, reset, and family communication when patterns continue.
Level 2Behavior that continues after support or requires additional adult involvement.Family partnership, counselor/dean/restorative support, MTSS review when appropriate, DBRC/CICO when appropriate, restorative repair, and in-school support when appropriate.
Level 3Behavior that significantly affects learning, safety, or the rights of others.Administrator involvement, formal documentation, family notification, restorative re-entry plan, possible in-school assignment, out-of-school suspension, or other response according to policy.
Level 4Serious behavior that threatens safety, substantially disrupts school, or may require district-level, charter-level, or law-enforcement review.Immediate administrative review, family notification, due process, possible suspension, expulsion referral, alternative placement consideration, or law-enforcement referral when required by law or policy.

Behavior Categories Families Should Know

The Student Code of Conduct includes expectations and possible responses for behavior such as:

  • Attendance concerns, tardiness, and cutting class
  • Dress code violations
  • Academic dishonesty
  • Technology misuse, unauthorized recording, inappropriate online activity, sexualized images, or digital conduct that affects the school community
  • Disrespectful, disruptive, or unsafe behavior
  • Refusal to follow reasonable adult directions
  • Bullying, harassment, intimidation, threats, or discrimination
  • Fighting, physical aggression, battery, or injury to others
  • Tobacco, nicotine, vaping, alcohol, drugs, controlled substances, or look-alike substances
  • Weapons, look-alike weapons, fireworks, or dangerous objects
  • Theft, vandalism, property damage, trespassing, or illegal conduct
  • Unsafe behavior on buses, at bus stops, during transportation, at school events, or during school-supervised activities

Due Process And Family Rights

When a serious behavior concern is reported, Matchbook will follow required due-process procedures before making a formal disciplinary decision. The school will gather relevant information, give the student an opportunity to explain what happened, review available evidence, and determine the appropriate response under the Student Code of Conduct.

For suspensions or other formal discipline, families will receive notice of the reason for the action, the length or nature of the action when applicable, any required next steps, and any appeal rights available under the applicable Code of Conduct, policy, or law.

No Student Sent Home Without Family Contact

No student will be dismissed early, sent home, or removed from campus for disciplinary reasons without parent/guardian contact and administrative authorization, unless an emergency, safety procedure, health procedure, or legal requirement applies.

Suspension, Re-Entry, And Return To Learning

Suspension is used only according to policy and due-process requirements. When a student returns from suspension or another significant removal, Matchbook will create a re-entry plan whenever appropriate. A re-entry plan may include:

  • The expectation the student is practicing
  • The repair or accountability step needed
  • The adult support assigned
  • Any academic work the student needs to access or complete
  • The family communication or meeting requested
  • The date the plan will be reviewed

Family partnership is expected after a significant disciplinary event. When a family meeting is needed, Matchbook will make reasonable efforts to schedule it promptly. Return-to-learning planning should protect safety, dignity, accountability, and access to instruction.

Students With Disabilities

Students with disabilities retain all protections required by IDEA, Section 504, Indiana Article 7, and school policy. Discipline decisions involving a student with an IEP, Section 504 plan, suspected disability, behavior support plan, or other protected status must follow required procedures — including team review, manifestation determination, service access, functional behavior assessment, or behavior intervention planning when needed. Nothing in this handbook limits a student's rights under an IEP, Section 504 plan, Article 7, or applicable law.

Family Partnership

Families are essential partners in student conduct and support. Families can help by reviewing expectations at home, keeping contact information current, attending meetings when requested, supporting re-entry plans, notifying the school when a student needs help, and contacting the school early when concerns arise. Matchbook's goal is always to return students to learning with safety, dignity, accountability, and support.

10Health, Safety & Student Services

Known, Safe
& Cared For

Immunizations

Indiana law requires up-to-date immunizations before attending school; records must be on file by Day 1. Religious or medical exemptions require written documentation each year.

Medications

All medications — including over-the-counter — are administered through the health office. Students may not self-administer unless a physician authorizes self-carry (e.g., inhalers, EpiPens) with written authorization.

Illness During the Day

An ill student is assessed by our on-site Nurse Practitioner / Physician Assistant. Families are notified and asked to pick up if needed — keep contact information current.

Searches & Lockers

Lockers and bags are school property and may be inspected at any time; students carry clear backpacks. A search of a student's person may occur with reasonable suspicion, conducted with dignity and respect.

Tobacco & Nicotine

Matchbook is a tobacco-free, nicotine-free campus. Possession or use of tobacco, nicotine, vaping devices, or e-cigarettes is prohibited and results in disciplinary consequences.

Bathroom Breaks & Lost and Found

Students get adequate restroom access throughout the day. Lost items are kept in the main office — please label belongings; unclaimed items are donated periodically.

Student Services

Counselors & Social Workers

Counselors support goal-setting, academic planning, and college/career readiness. Social workers support social-emotional well-being, peer and family challenges, attendance, and community resources. Families may reach out any time.

Multilingual Learners (MLL)

We celebrate linguistic diversity. Teachers are SIOP-trained; students receive bilingual support (iLitELL, Elevate) and newcomer services, assessed annually via WIDA ACCESS until proficiency 5.0.

Exceptional Learners

We serve all students, including those with disabilities, under IDEA and Indiana's Article 7, in the Least Restrictive Environment. Parents are key IEP partners — contact Cassie Pazos with questions.

Title IX & Mandatory Reporting

We prohibit discrimination on the basis of sex, including sexual orientation and gender identity. All staff must immediately report suspected sexual harassment, discrimination, or misconduct to the Title IX Coordinator; allegations meeting child abuse/neglect criteria are also reported to the Indiana Department of Child Services (DCS). Report via the main office or titleix@matchbooklearning.com. Both parties are entitled to due process; incidents involving sexual violence or nude images of a minor require an immediate police report.
